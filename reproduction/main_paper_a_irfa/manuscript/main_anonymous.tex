\title{Low-Dimensional Asset-Pricing Networks: Identification, Stability, and Economic Use}
\ifdefined\IRFAAUTHORED

\begin{abstract}
Compact asset-pricing representations need not identify a unique number of priced directions. We examine this distinction using U.S. equity networks, a known-loading simulation, and two bounded controls added after the initial study. In archived models, dimension rankings depend on test assets, loss weighting, seeds, data construction, and period. A later same-panel six-feature ablation does not reproduce the archived pipeline rank reversal: mean rank correlations are 0.65--0.72, and 11 of 12 primary paired intervals include zero. A locked 2020--2025 evaluation supports one of four specified directional criteria, with imprecise market-attenuation estimates. Legacy development overlaps January 2020, so this period is not an untouched research-wide holdout. In the simulation, noisy evaluation reduces average exact-dimension recovery from 64.2\% to 32.6\%; a finite nested Gaussian example explains persistent over-selection. Absolute activation rank declines as hidden layers narrow, while width-normalized rank increases. Training nevertheless lowers final-layer rank/width by 5.34 percentage points relative to matched random networks. Adding final-layer geometry improves held-out prediction of model-level pricing loss by 3.915\%, below the specified 5\% threshold. The later controls use familiar historical data and remain exploratory. The evidence supports reporting dimension rankings and uncertainty conditional on the payoff span, measurement, architecture, and criterion, rather than interpreting a validation minimum as an invariant economic dimension.
\end{abstract}
